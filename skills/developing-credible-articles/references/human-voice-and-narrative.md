# Human Voice and Narrative

## Purpose

Create writing that sounds like a specific human thinking from real material, not a model decorating an outline. These principles are generalized from strong conversational long-form writing; they do not authorize impersonation of any living writer.

## Voice-Material Gate

Before promising “the author's voice”, obtain at least one:

- a previous article or transcript;
- several messages written naturally by the author;
- confirmed phrases the author actually uses;
- a recorded or written account of the event in the author's own words.

Without this material, label the result `自然中文初稿，作者声音待校准`. Ask for a short sample before final voice polishing.

AI may organize evidence, propose analogies, find background, and expand an approved angle. The human must own first-hand observation, personal experience, emotional truth, core judgment, and final choice of angle.

## Topic Tension

Before drafting, check whether the topic contains at least two of these:

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Curiosity | Is there a contradiction, surprise, or unanswered question? |
| Knowledge | Will the reader learn a mechanism, fact, or useful distinction? |
| Recognition | Does it touch a situation or feeling the intended reader knows? |

If only one exists, improve the angle or gather better material rather than inflating the title.

## Choose the Article Form

| Available material | Article form | Natural movement |
|---|---|---|
| Author performed a real test | First-person experiment | question → action → failure → discovery → changed judgment |
| Author used a real product | Product experience | concrete task → use process → surprise/limit → who it fits |
| Public event plus deeper variable | Phenomenon analysis | observation → familiar explanation → contradiction → mechanism → boundary |
| Useful method grounded in practice | Method sharing | problem → principle → real example → action → learning cost/failure |
| Multiple verified sources or interviews | Investigation | question → evidence sequence → conflict → unresolved point → conclusion |

Do not force every article into a phenomenon-analysis template.

## Drafting Movements

Use these as movements, not mandatory sections:

1. Start from a confirmed event, action, object, sentence, or anomaly.
2. Let the author react only with an emotion or judgment they actually supplied.
3. Reveal the familiar explanation before challenging it fairly.
4. Move from scene to mechanism through a question the scene naturally raises.
5. Bring in evidence where the reader needs it; do not announce a lecture.
6. Return to the main line after each analogy or background detour.
7. Let the strongest countercase change the claim, not merely decorate it.
8. End by returning to an opening detail, now understood differently.

Optional cultural or historical elevation is allowed only when it clarifies the mechanism and has been verified. Remove it when it merely makes the ending sound grand.

## Rhythm

- Mix short and long sentences according to thought, not a fixed ratio.
- Allow an occasional one-sentence paragraph where the idea genuinely needs weight.
- Use questions to turn the argument, not to create fake suspense.
- Keep paragraphs short enough to read on a phone, but do not make every paragraph identical.
- A detour needs a brief return to the main question.
- When showing several cases or products, reveal them in an order that increases insight, not as a flat list.

Do not inject misspellings, repeated punctuation, profanity, forced slang, or verbal stumbles to imitate a person. Imperfection must come from honest uncertainty, not costume.

## Empathy and Opposition

State the opposing position in a form its strongest reasonable supporter would accept. Describe their constraints before disagreeing. Avoid “everyone thinks X, but they are wrong”. Prefer “I used to see it this way because...” only when the author confirmed that history.

A real case must be presented fairly. Do not remove inconvenient parts of the person's story. A profile inferred from a statistic is not a real person; either label it unmistakably as a hypothetical illustration or omit it.

## Four-Layer Human-Language Audit

### L1 Mechanical markers

Find and rewrite generic openings, automatic transitions, empty summaries, stacked abstractions, repeated “不是……而是……”, and unsupported rhetorical certainty. Common signals include:

- 在当今……时代 / 随着……发展
- 首先、其次、最后
- 值得注意的是 / 不难发现 / 综上所述
- 这意味着 / 本质上 / 换句话说
- 这件事让我重新想了一遍
- 真正重要的是 / 更需要警惕的是

Do not ban ordinary words globally. Rewrite only when they function as automatic scaffolding.

### L2 Narrative and rhythm

Check whether the opening is concrete, paragraphs vary naturally, detours return to the main line, and the ending completes an earlier detail. Locate the exact place where attention drops or the prose starts sounding like a report.

### L3 Content and fairness

Every important judgment needs a confirmed scene, source, or clearly marked inference. Knowledge should arrive where it explains the story. The opposing case must be represented fairly. Method articles must acknowledge cost, failure, and who should not use the method.

### L4 Author presence

Ask:

- Could this article have been written for any executive on the internet?
- Which sentences contain the author's actual observation or language?
- Is the emotion remembered, or merely named?
- Does the posture feel like a person sharing a judgment, a teacher lecturing, or a brand selling?

If no sentence requires this particular author, return to interview mode. Do not solve missing identity with borrowed catchphrases.

## Audit Output

Keep the report short:

```markdown
L1 机械AI痕迹：通过 / 需修改，位置...
L2 叙事与节奏：通过 / 需修改，断点...
L3 内容与公正：通过 / 需修改，缺口...
L4 作者存在感：通过 / 需补材料，需要...
```

Truth rules override style. A vivid sentence that contains invented detail must be removed, not polished.
