# Interview Map

## Purpose

Use this map to choose the next highest-value question. Do not read it aloud as a questionnaire. Ask one question, update the working state, then choose again.

## Question Priority

Ask the first unanswered question that could materially change the thesis or evidence plan:

1. What is the article trying to make the reader notice, reconsider, feel, or do?
2. Who is the reader, and what do they currently believe?
3. What concrete event or observation triggered the idea?
4. What is the familiar explanation?
5. What part of that explanation feels incomplete or wrong?
6. What deeper variable might explain the difference?
7. What real experience, person, failure, or detail does the author possess?
8. What evidence would make the author change their mind?
9. What is the strongest opposing explanation?
10. What conclusion is the author willing to defend publicly?

## Question Forms

Prefer:

- a single open question when the user has rich experience;
- 2-4 mutually exclusive choices when the user is exploring;
- a forced comparison when two explanations compete;
- a request for one concrete incident when answers remain abstract;
- a falsification question when conviction is high but evidence is weak.

Avoid:

- “请提供背景、目标、读者、案例和数据”；
- asking for information already supplied;
- questions whose answer cannot change the work;
- questions that merely invite agreement with the agent's angle;
- multiple decisions joined into one sentence.

Before sending, apply the one-field test:

```text
Which single working-state field will this answer update?
Can the user answer without also approving a consequence?
Did I append a fallback beginning with “如果没有/如果不能/否则”？
```

If the answer would update two fields, split it and send only the higher-value question.

## Human Material

Ask for material AI cannot legitimately invent:

- what the author personally saw, did, heard, or got wrong;
- the exact moment their view changed;
- a real person's actions, with permission and privacy considered;
- discomfort, doubt, conflict, or failed attempt;
- phrases the author naturally uses;
- a counterexample they still cannot explain.

Do not demand emotional disclosure. Let the user exclude private material.

## Resume Behavior

When resuming a long conversation:

1. show at most five lines: confirmed thesis, evidence state, open decision, strongest objection, next phase;
2. ask whether any of those changed only if there is evidence of conflict;
3. continue with one question rather than restarting intake.
