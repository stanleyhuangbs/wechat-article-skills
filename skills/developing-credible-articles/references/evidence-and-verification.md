# Evidence and Verification

## Claim Ledger

Track each material claim with these fields:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Claim | One atomic, publishable proposition |
| Type | fact, experience, inference, analogy, opinion, forecast, unresolved |
| Source | original URL, document, dataset, or named interviewee |
| Authority | why this source can know the claim |
| Date | publication and relevant event period |
| Scope | population, geography, product, period, and denominator |
| Method | measurement, sample, comparison, and uncertainty when relevant |
| Incentive | sponsor, vendor, institution, advocacy, or other interest |
| Conflict | supporting, conflicting, or absent evidence |
| Confidence | high, medium, low, or unresolved |
| Treatment | publish, attribute, qualify, research, or remove |

## Source Hierarchy

Use the closest source to the fact:

1. original dataset, law, filing, transcript, study, product documentation, or direct interview;
2. authoritative analysis that exposes method and limitations;
3. reputable reporting that links or names its evidence;
4. secondary commentary for context only;
5. social posts, snippets, reposts, and AI summaries as leads only.

Higher rank does not guarantee truth. A primary source may be biased, badly measured, or outside the claim's scope.

## Verification Rules

### Numbers

Confirm exact value, unit, denominator, period, geography, sample, adjustment, and whether it is gross or net. Preserve uncertainty and confidence intervals when material.

### Quotations

Match exact words against the original recording, transcript, document, or post. Check speaker, date, context, and translation. If exact matching is unavailable, paraphrase without quotation marks and attribute the idea.

### Personal Cases

Confirm who provided the account, what was directly observed, what is recollection, and whether publication permission or anonymization is needed. One case illustrates; it does not establish prevalence.

### Causal Claims

Ask what changed, compared with what, over which period, and what alternative causes were controlled or ruled out. If causal identification is weak, describe association or sequence instead.

### Current Claims

Verify facts that can change, including executives, prices, rankings, user counts, laws, product capabilities, policies, schedules, and market status, at the time of writing.

### Conflicting Sources

Do not average conclusions or choose the preferred source. Compare definitions, samples, periods, methods, incentives, and measured outcomes. State whether the conflict is real or whether the sources answer different questions.

## Minimum Publication Standard

A material factual claim can be published only when:

- the source has been opened and inspected;
- the source actually supports the sentence's wording and scope;
- date and definition fit the article;
- conflicts and limitations are disclosed when they could change interpretation;
- the claim is no stronger than the evidence.

Otherwise attribute, qualify, research, or remove it.

## Research Log

Keep short records:

```text
Claim:
Query or source path:
Source inspected:
What it supports:
What it does not support:
Conflict or limitation:
Publication treatment:
```

Do not pad an article with sources. Research exists to test the thesis, not decorate it.
